01 · Orientation

01How Daubert is put together

Level 1
Organization
Your firm. Holds shared things: expert profiles, reusable boilerplate, the member list.
›
Level 2
Case
One matter. Geffen v. Sun. Has its own members, files and deliverables.
›
Level 3
Investigation
One line of inquiry inside the case. A case can hold many.
›
Level 4
Trace
The actual diagram: wallets as nodes, transfers as arrows.

Running alongside all of it: the Data Room (the case's file cabinet) and Productions (the things you hand to counsel: reports, charts, chronologies, declarations and redlines).

What you will actually do on a matter

  1. Open the case Counsel or a colleague adds you. It shows up on your home screen.
  2. Seed a trace Paste the wallet addresses at issue. Daubert pulls their transaction history and draws the diagram.
  3. Work the graph Label the wallets you can identify, expand the flows that matter, hide the noise.
  4. Ask the AI It reads the same case data you can see, and can run the tedious parts for you.
  5. Produce Turn findings into a report, a chart, a chronology or a signed declaration.
  6. Export PDF or Word, with jurisdiction-correct formatting. Bundle several items into one exhibit.
The one thing to remember

Daubert never invents on-chain facts. Every wallet, amount and timestamp on a trace was fetched from a public blockchain and can be clicked through to the block explorer. Your labels and your narrative are the only interpretive layer, and exactly the part you will be cross-examined on.

02 · Access

02Signing in, and who can see what

Daubert lives at app.dauberts.ai. Sign in with Google, Microsoft, or a one-time code sent to your email.

Read this before you sign in

Sign in with the exact email address your invite was issued to.

Your invite is a single-use link tied to one specific email address. If you sign in with any other address, even another address of your own, Daubert will refuse the invite and tell you which address it belongs to.

So if the link was issued to jane.doe@yourfirm.com, sign in with that Google, Microsoft or email-code identity. Not a personal Gmail. Not an alias.

How invites actually reach you

Daubert does not send invitation emails. Whoever adds you generates a link and sends it to you themselves, usually by email or chat. The link carries your role and expires.

04 · Your profile

04Your declarant profile

Build your expert profile once at the firm level and every declaration in every case can pull your qualifications in with a click. This is the single highest-leverage thing to do on day one.

Three ways to build it

OptionWhat happens
Insert manuallyYou type the profile: name, title, firm, qualification paragraphs, rate, prior testimony.
Pull from CVUpload your CV as a PDF (up to 10 MB). Daubert extracts your credentials into draft qualification paragraphs.
Pull from previous declarationUpload a declaration you have already filed. Daubert lifts the qualifications section, which is usually the cleanest source.
Nothing is saved until you approve it

Extraction produces a draft shown to you in a review step. Read every line before saving. It leaves fields blank rather than guessing.

The boilerplate library

Beneath the roster sits a library of reusable paragraphs shared by the whole firm: the chain primer you write for a lay judge, the exhibit-authentication language you use every time. Save it once, insert it into any declaration. Inserted copies are independent, so editing one does not touch the original.

Who can edit what

You can always edit your own profile. Firm admins can edit anyone's. Tick "This is my profile" when you create yours so it links to your account.

05 · The workspace

05The three-panel workspace

Every case opens into the same layout. Both side panels can be dragged wider or collapsed with the small arrows at their edges.

Left
The case

Investigations and their traces, all productions grouped by type, the Data Room, and your saved scripts. Click anything to open it in the center.

Center
The canvas

The trace diagram, or whichever production you opened. Nodes are wallets, arrows are transfers, boxes are groups you drew.

Right
The assistant

Case-scoped chat. It can read the graph, the files and the productions, and, if you are an editor, change them.

Reading a trace

On screenMeans
NodeA wallet address. Its default shape follows what kind of address Daubert thinks it is: an ordinary wallet, a smart contract, and a known exchange each get a different one.
ArrowA transfer, or a bundle of transfers, from one wallet to another. Labeled with amount and date range.
Box around nodesA group you drew to hold a narrative chapter, such as "Sun's Purchase, Nov 2021". You give it the title and the color.
Free-floating textA label you placed on the canvas: a caption, an annotation, or an off-chain fact that did not come from a blockchain.
Appearance is your choice, not the data's

Nothing about how a node looks is derived from the money. Color, size and shape are all set by hand, and any of them can be overridden on any node. A large red circle is large and red because somebody made it so, not because the amounts are big.

Use that deliberately. Styling is part of your narrative, so be ready to explain every choice you made in it.

Investigations vs. traces

An investigation is a line of inquiry, such as "NFT Token Ownership". Inside it, traces are separate diagrams or layers you can show and hide independently, so one investigation can carry the deposits, the withdrawals and the sale as three toggleable views of the same story. Use the eye icon beside each trace to hide it.

06 · Tracing

06Tracing the money

Everything on a trace starts as a fetch from a public blockchain. You choose what to pull, then you choose what it means.

Chains Daubert can trace

EthereumPolygon ArbitrumBase TronBitcoin Solana

Pulling transactions

Narrow before you pull

A busy exchange wallet can have hundreds of thousands of transactions. Pulling all of them is slow and buries the story in noise. Set a date range around the events at issue and keep the limit modest. You can always fetch again.

Not sure how to scope it? Ask the AI. Describe what you are looking for in plain language, something like "this wallet is enormous, I only care about transfers to Sotheby's around November 2021, what range should I pull?", and it will work out the bounds and can run the fetch for you.

Labeling: where your expertise shows

A hex string means nothing to a judge. "Sotheby's" or "Justin Sun Public Wallet" means everything. Daubert also carries a registry of known entities (exchanges, bridges, mixers, well-known public wallets) that the assistant can look up for you. Anything it cannot identify stays unlabeled until you can support the identification.

Write down your reasoning

Use the Notes field on every wallet you label to record why: a public attribution, a produced document, an exchange subpoena response. When you are asked on the stand how you knew wallet X was the defendant's, that note is your answer.

Shaping the graph

Look forWhat it doesWhere
Bundle IN Collapses every incoming transfer from the selected wallets into one summarizing arrow, so dozens of small movements stop crowding the diagram. Address Details panel
Bundle OUT The same for outgoing transfers. Address Details panel
Group within trace Select several nodes, name the group, and Daubert draws a titled box around them. Each group becomes a chapter of your narrative. Panel that appears when you select more than one node
Hide / Show Turns a whole trace off so you can show one flow at a time. The icon flips to a crossed-out eye once hidden. Beside each trace in the sidebar, and on right-click

Choosing a model

The dropdown at the top of the chat picks the underlying model. The default is the strongest and is the right choice for analysis and drafting. Lighter models are faster and cheaper for simple lookups and reformatting.

You are the expert, it is not

Read every paragraph the assistant drafts before it goes anywhere near a filing. Check the numbers against the graph and the graph against the block explorer. You sign the declaration; the assistant does not. Treat its output as a competent associate's first draft: useful, and never final.

If you are a viewer

On cases where your role is Viewer the assistant runs in read-only mode. It will answer questions but will not modify the case. The chat box tells you so.

10 · Declarations

10Drafting a declaration

A declaration in Daubert is not a word processor document. It is a structured object (caption, sections, numbered paragraphs, exhibits, execution block) that gets rendered into your jurisdiction's format on the way out.

Pick your jurisdiction

FormatAuthorityRendering
CaliforniaC.C.P. § 2015.528-line pleading paper with the numbered gutter and per-page footer. The default.
New YorkC.P.L.R. 2106Double-spaced affirmation with a bordered caption box.
Federal28 U.S.C. § 1746Generic federal unsworn declaration.
TexasCiv. Prac. & Rem. Code § 132.001Jurat reciting date of birth and address, as the statute requires.
FloridaFla. Stat. § 92.525Generic Florida declaration.
Confirm before you file

The California and New York formats are modelled on filed documents. The Federal, Texas and Florida formats are careful best-effort renderings. Whichever you use, have filing counsel confirm the caption and local rules for the specific court before anything is filed.

Exhibits

Add exhibits from the Exhibits panel and Daubert labels them A, B, C… automatically. Attach an exhibit to a paragraph and the citation stays correct even if you reorder. Each exhibit records where it came from: a transaction, a URL, a file, or something else.

You are left editing rather than starting from a blank page, which is where your judgment is actually worth something.

11 · Redlining

11Redlining a document

Counsel sends you a brief, a letter or a draft declaration and asks whether the crypto facts are right. Redline is built for exactly that.

How it works

  1. Put the draft in the Data Room Word or PDF. Word gives you the better result.
  2. Create a redline production from it Daubert takes a permanent, verbatim snapshot of the text. That snapshot never changes.
  3. Let the assistant review it It reads the draft, checks every factual and on-chain claim against your case record, and proposes small, precisely anchored edits, each with a stated basis.
  4. Triage every proposal Accept, Reject or Modify. Nothing is applied until you say so.
  5. Export Word writes your accepted edits onto the original file as real tracked changes, with a margin comment carrying the reason for each.

Why the snapshot matters

The original text is frozen at the moment the redline is created and cannot be edited, replaced or converted into another kind of production. Every proposed edit is anchored to a verbatim quotation from that frozen text. This is what makes the redline defensible: there is no way for the underlying document to have quietly shifted beneath the changes you approved.

Edits versus reviewer notes

KindUse it for
Anchored editA specific span of text that is wrong and can be fixed by replacing, deleting, or inserting after it.
Reviewer noteRisk flags and open questions that are not a text change, such as "confirm this $141M figure against Ex. 15 before filing".
Two things to know before exporting

Word export needs at least one accepted edit, and needs the source file still to be in the Data Room. If the source was a PDF there are no original Word bytes to mark up, so the export is a reconstruction and its filename says so. PDF export always works and renders only the edits you accepted.

PDF vs Word for declarations

The PDF is the faithful one. It carries the pleading gutter and per-page line numbers. Word cannot represent a fixed gutter, so the .docx comes out without it. Send Word when counsel needs to edit; file the PDF.

Building an exhibit bundle

The + Exhibit button at the bottom of the sidebar assembles several items into one document. Each becomes a page in the final PDF.

A useful default bundle

Graph snapshot → chronology → chart → summary report. It walks a reader from the picture, to the dated record, to the trend, to the argument, in the order they need it.

13Connect your own AI (BYOA)

The assistant inside Daubert is not the only way to work. You can point the AI you already use, Claude or ChatGPT, straight at your Daubert cases. That is what "bring your own AI" means.

Why you might want this

One place to work

Ask about your case in the same window where you are already drafting, reading and researching.

Bring your own context

Your AI can combine Daubert case data with documents and tools it already has access to.

Longer jobs

Set it on a large, repetitive task and let it work through it against the real case data.

Same rules apply

An outside AI gets exactly your permissions on exactly your cases, never more.

Both routes below ask you for the same two values. Have them ready.

FieldValue
NameDaubert
MCP server URLhttps://api.dauberts.ai/mcp  (keep the /mcp on the end)

Claude

  1. Open your connectors Customize → Connectors. The old claude.ai/settings/connectors address is retired and now only tells you they moved.
  2. Add a custom connector Click +, then Add custom connector, and fill in the two fields above. Leave the Advanced settings empty: Daubert registers itself, so there is no client ID to find.
  3. Sign in and pick your organization Claude opens a browser tab. Sign in to Daubert, choose which organization to grant, and approve.
  4. Allow the tools once, not every time Open the connector you just added, find Tool permissions, and set them to Always allow.
Do not skim step 4

Claude's approval dialog puts Allow once in the obvious white button and Always allow in the quieter one above it. The path of least resistance, clicking the obvious button or pressing Cmd+Return, is the one that makes Claude ask again on every single call, forever.

On a Claude Team or Enterprise plan?

This applies to Claude only. Your Claude workspace administrator has to register Daubert once under Organization settings → Connectors. After that you will see a Connect button on the entry they created, and you can skip straight to signing in and approving.

ChatGPT

ChatGPT will not accept a custom MCP server until developer mode is on. That is the step people miss, so it is first. Note that its panel is called Plugins, not Connectors.

  1. Turn on developer mode Settings → Plugins → Advanced. Custom MCP connectors do not appear until this is on.
  2. Create a connector Click Create and fill in the two fields above.
  3. Sign in and pick your organization Same as Claude: a browser tab opens, you sign in and approve.
  4. Approve the tools as they come up ChatGPT asks before each write. Choose the option that remembers your approval.
ChatGPT asks more often, and that is ChatGPT

ChatGPT has no standing pre-approval. Its confirmation remembers a tool for the current conversation only, and a new conversation starts asking from scratch. Claude can pre-approve a whole group at once. If the asking becomes tiresome, that is the surface, not Daubert.

GroupWhat it covers

The guardrails

  • Scoped to one organization. You choose which firm to grant when you approve. It cannot reach any other.
  • Bounded by your role. Your permissions are re-checked on every single request, not once at sign-in. If your access changes, its access changes with it, immediately.
  • Every change is logged. Each create or update is recorded with the AI, the organization, the action, the target and whether it succeeded or was denied.
  • File reads are logged too. When a connected AI reads a Data Room file it lands in the same chain-of-custody log as a human download.
  • Revocable in one click. The bin icon beside a connected AI cuts it off instantly.

Your audit trail

The AI activity table on your Account page lists every change any connected AI has made on your behalf, including ones you have since disconnected. Check it after a long session; it is the fastest way to see exactly what changed.

The judgment call

A connected AI has real write access to real matters. Give it narrow, specific instructions, review the AI activity log afterwards, and revoke connections you are no longer using. If you are unsure whether an outside AI should be allowed to touch a case, do that work in the built-in assistant instead. It is scoped to one case at a time by design.

A good first task

Ask your connected AI: "List my Daubert cases, then summarize the investigations and productions in the one called X." It is read-only, it proves the connection works, and it shows you the shape of what it can see.

14 · Reference

14Habits that hold up under cross

  • Label with a reason. Every wallet you name gets a note saying how you identified it. Unsupported attributions are the softest part of any crypto expert's testimony.
  • Verify before you assert. Every number that reaches a declaration should be traceable from the paragraph, to the graph, to the block explorer. The chronology's source links exist for exactly this.
  • Do not let the AI have the last word. Read every drafted paragraph. It is a fast, well-informed associate, not a signatory.
  • Keep the source documents in the Data Room. That is where the chain-of-custody log lives. Files on your desktop have none.
  • Say what you did not do. Scope limits belong in the declaration, not in your head.
  • Re-read the preview before exporting. Preview is the real rendering. Two minutes there beats a corrected filing.

Glossary

Address / wallet
A public account on a blockchain, written as a long string of letters and numbers. A node on your graph.
Chain
The specific blockchain a transaction happened on. Daubert traces seven: Ethereum, Polygon, Arbitrum, Base, Tron, Bitcoin and Solana.
Transaction hash
The unique identifier of a single transfer. Paste one into the search bar to find it; click one in a chronology to open the block explorer.
Block explorer
The public website for a chain: Etherscan, Tronscan, mempool.space, Solscan. The independent source anyone can check your work against.
Bundle
Many small transfers collapsed into one summarizing arrow, so the diagram stays readable.
Trace
One diagram inside an investigation. Traces can be shown and hidden independently.
Production
Anything you hand to counsel: report, chart, chronology, declaration or redline.
Declarant
The expert signing a declaration. That is you. Your profile is stored once at firm level and reused across cases.
Data Room
The case's file store, with an access log on every upload, download and deletion.
Exhibit
Either a labeled item attached to a declaration (A, B, C…), or a bundle of several productions exported as one PDF.
BYOA (bring your own AI)
Connecting an outside AI, Claude or ChatGPT, to your Daubert cases, with your permissions and a full audit trail.
